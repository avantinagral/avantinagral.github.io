The first eight weeks of gigs

How many people are responding

  • Creatives: interest is high, email replies are low. 388 creatives raised their hand for gigs and were sent the new "apply in your dashboard" steps, and 310 commented on Instagram asking about gigs. Direct email replies to the invites were about 28 out of ~920, roughly 3%. That's expected, because the invites point people to apply in the dashboard, not to reply.
  • What the replies said. Most were "thanks, I'll check it out" or "applied". About a third asked a real question (see below), and a handful declined, usually over location or timing.
  • Clients: fast, engaged. AutoGram, Superliving and Evensong each clicked through the matched profiles within minutes to hours of receiving them. AutoGram came back to its matches two weeks and a month later. Verona replied the same day at nearly every step.
  • Applications themselves happen in the dashboard, so the number of people who actually hit "apply" isn't visible in email. The dashboard is the place to check that count.

What people are asking

  • "Can I do this remotely, or from where I live?" Most common On-site roles drew questions from people in other cities and other countries, including Nigeria and South Africa. Some preferred to work in their hometown. The usual answer was to point them to the remote video-editing gig.
  • "How does this work?" People asked to have the steps explained and where exactly to apply, which is what the "one quick step" and dashboard emails now spell out.
  • "Am I experienced enough?" Questions like "can I start as an intern first?" and "I haven't worked at scale, should I still apply?". The answer: yes, the company looks at your work and how you think.
  • "What's the role and the pay?" People asked for the brief. The Founding Marketing Lead brief (full-time, Mumbai, founding team) included the salary range.
  • "Can my company post a gig?" Some people asked how to list their own openings and were pointed to the hiring form on the website.
  • Timing and fit. A few missed a deadline while on a shoot, and one passed on a fixed-budget gig because the cost and time didn't work for them.
